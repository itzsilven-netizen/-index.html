# Cold Call Masterclass — AI Agency Edition

A full system for booking meetings via cold calling: strategy, lead sourcing, prep, the script, delivery, objections, and tracking — built from Micah's 10-meetings-in-10-hours process and Alex Hormozi's cold-calling principles, adapted for an AI agency (receptionists, chatbots, websites) selling to service-based industries.

---

## 1. The Mindset

- **Volume negates luck.** Until you've built real skill (roughly your first ~10,000 reps), don't freestyle — stick to the script. More dials in less time beats a "perfect" call ratio.
- **Belief drives delivery.** You have to believe the service actually helps before your tone will convince anyone else it does.
- **Set a hard number and a deadline.** Micah didn't aim for "some meetings" — he committed to 10 in 10 hours. A concrete target changes how you dial. Pick your own number (e.g., "5 booked meetings today") before you start.

---

## 2. Building the Lead List ("The Hit List")

Before dialing, build a targeted list — don't call cold into a random directory.

1. **Pick your ICP (Ideal Customer Profile).** For an AI agency selling receptionists/chatbots/websites: owners, office managers, or ops managers at service-based businesses (contractors, med/dental offices, law firms, salons, real estate, home services) — anyone who loses revenue to missed calls or slow response.
2. **Source contacts.** Use a B2B contact database (ZoomInfo, Apollo, LinkedIn Sales Navigator) filtered to:
   - Industry / vertical (your chosen niche)
   - Title: owner, office manager, operations manager
   - **Prioritize mobile numbers** — they answer far more often than office lines.
3. **Export and filter in a spreadsheet.** Sort so mobile-number contacts are called first; keep a status column (Not Called / Left VM / Booked / Not Interested / Bad Number).
4. **Build in volume.** Load 3x more contacts than your target number of meetings — expect a low answer rate (Micah's real session: 802 dials → 91 pickups → 19 meetings, about a 2.4% dial-to-meeting rate).

---

## 3. Pre-Call Prep (do this once, before the session — not per call)

- **Get your mind right.** A few minutes of quiet, tea/water, deep breathing — whatever gets you calm and focused before the first dial.
- **Rehearse the script out loud** at least once, full volume, before you start — not silently in your head.
- **Have your calendar open** and ready to book on the spot — never say "I'll follow up to schedule," always book live on the call.

### Per-call prep (5 minutes, before each key call — optional for high volume, worth it for top targets)
- Quick look at the business (what they do, how long they've been open, any recent news).
- Note anything specific you can reference in the call — even one detail personalizes the pitch and boosts trust.

## 4. The Script

See `cold-call-script.md` (and the published artifact) for the full word-for-word script: opener, credibility/problem/solution pitch, call-to-action, objection handling, and close. Keep the **opener fixed** across every call — that's your 7-second trust bridge and shouldn't change call to call.

---

## 5. Delivery — How You Say It Matters More Than What You Say

- **85% delivery, 15% words.** Tone, pace, pauses, and energy carry most of the call's effectiveness — not the exact wording.
- **Don't rush the pauses.** Especially right after the opener's "...relevant or not" — that beat hands control back to the prospect and is what keeps them on the line.
- **Record your best calls ("game tape").** After a strong call, note what your tone/energy was like. Before your next session (or when you hit a slump), replay it in your head or listen back to recreate that energy.
- **Match their energy.** Rushed prospect → keep it tight. Relaxed prospect → let it breathe a little more.

---

## 6. Objection Handling

Already built into `cold-call-script.md` — four core objections and short, low-friction responses that redirect back to the meeting ask:
- "Not interested / already have someone"
- "Just send me info"
- "How much does this cost?"
- "I'm slammed / bad timing" → use the **calendar-invite tactic**: send the invite directly so the prospect doesn't have to actively schedule — they only need to reschedule if something conflicts.

---

## 7. Tracking Your Session

Log these live, per calling session, to know your real numbers (not guesses):

| Metric | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **Total Dials** | Volume — are you calling enough? |
| **Pickups** | Answer rate — is your list/timing right? |
| **Conversations (right ICP)** | Are you reaching decision-makers, not gatekeepers/wrong numbers? |
| **Meetings Booked** | Your actual output |
| **Conversion Rate** (meetings ÷ conversations) | Is your pitch/close working once you get someone on the line? |

**Reference benchmark** (Micah's 10-hour session): 802 dials, 91 pickups, 62 right-ICP conversations, 19 meetings booked, 30.6% conversion (booked ÷ conversations). Use this as a rough sanity check, not a hard target — your numbers will vary by list quality and niche.

---

## 8. Session Structure (put it all together)

1. Build/refresh your hit list (target 3x your meeting goal in dial-ready contacts).
2. Prep: rehearse script out loud, calendar open, mind right.
3. Dial in blocks — track live using the metrics table above.
4. After every booked meeting, immediately confirm with a calendar invite (don't wait).
5. At the halfway point, check your conversion rate — if it's low, it's a list-quality or delivery problem, not a "cold calling doesn't work" problem.
6. After the session, review what your best calls sounded like (game tape) so your next session starts at that energy, not from zero.
